## Key Question 1 — Timeline of key results: exact complexity, key idea, verified bibliography; who was first to O(n)/O(1), who was first to 2n+o(n) bits

### Takeaway
The first O(n)-preprocessing, O(1)-query RMQ came from **Gabow, Bentley & Tarjan (STOC 1984)**: they reduce RMQ to LCA on the Cartesian tree (Vuillemin 1980) and combine this with **Harel & Tarjan's (1984) linear-time/O(1) LCA**. It uses O(n) words, i.e. O(n log n) bits. Berkman–Vishkin (1993) made the LCA side practical by reducing it to ±1RMQ. Bender & Farach-Colton (2000) say explicitly that they did not invent a new algorithm: theirs is a sequential re-engineering of an existing PRAM algorithm. **2n+o(n) bits** came in two steps. The first was Fischer & Heun (ESCAPE 2007), a *systematic* scheme: 2n+o(n) bits *plus the input array A*, which is still read at query time. The first *non-systematic (encoding)* scheme with 2n+o(n) bits and O(1) query was **Fischer, LATIN 2010** (arXiv Dec 2008). Its journal version is Fischer & Heun, SICOMP 2011, with 2n+O(n lg lg n/lg n) bits. Before that, the only encoding was **Sadakane's 4n+o(n) bits (J. Discrete Algorithms 2007)**.

### Cited Findings

#### A. Who was first to O(n) preprocessing / O(1) query (be careful with attribution)
- Fischer & Heun (SICOMP 2011) write that "it was noted already a quarter of a century ago [Gabow et al. 1984] that a scheme of size O(n) words suffices to answer RMQs in optimal constant time". The scheme transforms RMQ into LCA on the Cartesian tree [Vuillemin 1980]. "For constant-time LCA-queries, linear preprocessing schemes had already been discovered earlier [Harel–Tarjan]." — [Fischer & Heun SICOMP 2011 preprint, KIT](https://ae.iti.kit.edu/1810.php)
- Fischer (LATIN 2010): "The historically first such scheme due to Gabow et al. [GBT84] is based on the following idea: because an RMQ-instance can be transformed into an instance of LCAs in the Cartesian Tree, one can use any linear-time preprocessing scheme for O(1)-LCAs." — [Fischer, Optimal Succinctness for RMQ, arXiv:0812.2775](https://arxiv.org/abs/0812.2775)
- Liu & Yu (STOC 2020): RMQ "has a classic solution using O(n) space and O(1) query time by Gabow, Bentley, Tarjan [GBT84] and Harel, Tarjan [HT84]". — [Liu & Yu, arXiv:2004.05738](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05738)
- Fischer's thesis says that the Euler-tour connection between LCA on a tree T and RMQ on its depth array was "first discovered by Gabow et al. (1984)". The thesis also says Gabow et al. showed the converse reduction RMQ→LCA via Cartesian trees, "thus making the two problems linearly equivalent". However, "as they could not directly solve RMQ-instances … they could not use RMQ to improve on constant-time LCA-computation. … this step was made by Berkman and Vishkin (1993)". BV93 noticed that the Euler-tour reduction produces the restricted ±1RMQ and "presented a direct algorithm for this restricted version". — [J. Fischer, PhD thesis, LMU 2007, §3.3.1 (PDF)](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/7505/1/Fischer_Johannes.pdf)
- Harel & Tarjan (1984) abstract: on a RAM with uniform cost and Ω(log n) bits per word, the algorithm answers each query in O(1) time after O(n) preprocessing (static trees). They also prove that "any pointer machine that solves this problem requires Ω(log log n) time per query". — [Harel & Tarjan, SIAM J. Comput. 13(2) (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1137/0213024)
- Schieber & Vishkin (1988) abstract: linear-time and linear-space preprocessing with O(1) query, "as in Harel and Tarjan". Its advantage is being "simple and easily parallelizable": O(log n) time with an optimal number of processors on an EREW PRAM. — [Schieber & Vishkin, SIAM J. Comput. 17(6) (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1137/0217079)
- Berkman & Vishkin (1993) abstract: introduces the "recursive star-tree" parallel data structure. It gives O(α(n)) time with an optimal number of processors on the CRCW PRAM (α = inverse Ackermann). Applications include "finding lowest common ancestors in trees by a new algorithm that is considerably simpler than the known algorithms". — [Berkman & Vishkin, SIAM J. Comput. 22(2) (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1137/0222017)
- Bender & Farach-Colton (author preprint dated May 16, 2000) say in so many words: "Our point is not to present a new algorithm. Indeed, we have already noted that this algorithm has appeared as a PRAM algorithm before". Their algorithm is "a sequentialization of a previously known PRAM algorithm of Berkman, Breslauer, Galil, Schieber, and Vishkin [STOC 1989, 'Highly parallelizable problems']". This preprint cites only three references (BBGSV89, Harel–Tarjan, Schieber–Vishkin). It does not cite Gabow–Bentley–Tarjan or Vuillemin. — [Bender & Farach-Colton preprint PDF](https://www3.cs.stonybrook.edu/~bender/newpub/BenderFa00-lca.pdf)
- Fischer's thesis instead credits the "Berkman-Vishkin algorithm" (BV93 combined with Gabow et al. 1984). It says it follows "the presentation from Bender et al. (2005) who rediscovered and simplified the algorithm". — [Fischer thesis §3.4.1](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/7505/1/Fischer_Johannes.pdf)

#### B. Who was first to 2n + o(n) bits (systematic vs. non-systematic)
- Before 2007, every O(1)-RMQ scheme used O(n lg n) bits, i.e. O(n) words. "The only existing [non-systematic] scheme is due to Sadakane [J. Discrete Algorithms 2007] and uses 4n+o(n) bits". It is built on the balanced-parentheses sequence of the Cartesian tree plus "n 'fake' leaves" (more precisely 4n + O(n lg² lg n / lg n) bits). It needs O(n lg n) bits of construction space. — [Fischer & Heun SICOMP 2011 preprint, §1.1.2 and Table 1.1](https://ae.iti.kit.edu/1810.php)
- Brodal, Davoodi & Rao's table of 1D results gives four rows. Fischer–Heun ESCAPE 2007 [12]: 2n+o(n)+|A| bits, uses the Cartesian tree but not LCA, **accesses A**. Sadakane [16]: 4n+o(n), **does not access A**. Fischer LATIN 2010 [11]: 2n+o(n), no access to A, "introducing a new data structure named 2d-Min-Heap instead of using the Cartesian tree". — [Brodal, Davoodi & Rao, ESA 2010 preprint](https://cs.au.dk/~gerth/papers/esa10.pdf)
- Fischer's LATIN 2010 Table 1 lists [Fischer–Heun ESCAPE 2007] with final space "2n + o(n) + |A|". It lists Sadakane's 4n+o(n) as the "only non-systematic data structure so far", and its own result as "2n + o(n) … final space requirement optimal". The abstract adds that the scheme "can be computed in linear time, using only n + o(n) additional bits for construction". — [Fischer, arXiv:0812.2775 (v3 Dec 2009)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0812.2775); same text in [KIT copy](https://ae.iti.kit.edu/1610.php)
- Fischer's thesis (oral exam 8 Oct 2007), Theorem 3.11: time ⟨O(n), O(1)⟩ and bit-space ⟦2n+o(n), 2n+o(n)⟧. The scheme is systematic: the bits are additional to A, which is read for minimum comparisons. The thesis's repository abstract says the space "is shown to be asymptotically optimal in a general setting". — [Fischer thesis PDF](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/7505/1/Fischer_Johannes.pdf); [LMU edoc record 7505](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/7505/)
- **Correction to that claim:** Fischer & Heun's SICOMP footnote 3 says "The claimed lower bound of 2n+o(n)+|A| bits under the 'min-probe-model' [ESCAPE 2007] turned out to be wrong, as was kindly pointed out to the authors by S. Srinivasa Rao (personal communication, November 2007)". — [Fischer & Heun SICOMP preprint](https://ae.iti.kit.edu/1810.php). Brodal–Davoodi–Rao: "Fischer and Heun claim 2n − o(n) bits lower bound for the additional space, however their proof is incorrect" (refuted by their Theorem 2). — [BDR ESA 2010](https://cs.au.dk/~gerth/papers/esa10.pdf)
- Liu & Yu cite the 2n bound loosely: "the space is further improved to 2n + O(n log log n/log n) by Fischer and Heun [FH07, FH11]". — [Liu & Yu arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05738). (Inference: per BDR's table and Fischer's own LATIN 2010 table, the *encoding* version of this bound is LATIN 2010 / SICOMP 2011, not ESCAPE 2007.)
- Ferrada & Navarro: "Fischer and Heun [SICOMP 2011] proposed the first RMQ data structure … that uses 2n + o(n) bits and answers queries in O(1) time without accessing A". It "converts the Cartesian tree of A into a general tree, which is represented using DFUDS". — [Ferrada & Navarro, Improved RMQ (preprint PDF)](https://www.dcc.uchile.cl/~gnavarro/ps/jda16.pdf)

#### C. What each Fischer–Heun paper achieved (from SICOMP 2011, the consolidated version)
- SICOMP 2011 is the journal version combining CPM 2006, ESCAPE 2007, DCC 2008 and LATIN 2010 ("Parts of this work have already been presented at …"). Its results:
  - (1) **Systematic** (A available at query time): 2n/c(n) − Θ(n lg lg n/(c(n) lg n)) bits on top of A with O(c(n)) query time, for any c(n) = O(n^ε). With c(n)=O(1) this is "the first systematic scheme with linear bit-complexity", and the trade-off "is optimal [Brodal et al.]".
  - (2) **Compressed:** nH_k + O(n(k lg σ + lg lg n)/lg n) + |A| bits with O(1) query.
  - (3) **Non-systematic:** 2n + O(n lg lg n / lg n) bits, O(1) query (Thm 5.8). With Sadakane–Navarro's ±1rmq it becomes 2n + O(n/polylg n) (Cor. 5.9).
  - "All data structures can be constructed in linear time and almost in-place."
  — [Fischer & Heun SICOMP preprint](https://ae.iti.kit.edu/1810.php)
- Fischer–Heun CPM 2006, as described on the Springer listing summarized by search: a direct general-RMQ method with "linear preprocessing time and constant query time, with no dynamic data structure involved". It "consumes less than half of the space that is needed by the method by Berkman and Vishkin" and is "almost twice as fast in practice". Applications include LCA on binary trees and an array-only O(1) LCE. — [Springer chapter DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/11780441_5) (abstract wording via search summary; I could not fetch the page directly. Treat the wording as UNVERIFIED-verbatim.)

#### D. Other results in the timeline
- Davoodi, Navarro, Raman & Rao (2014) abstract: an encoding with 2n+o(n) bits and O(1) worst-case query, which "matches the previous result of Fischer and Heun, but is obtained in a more natural way". It encodes a *random* array in 1.919n+o(n) bits in expectation. For range top-2 queries (RT2Q) it uses 3.272n+o(n) bits with O(1) time, plus effective-entropy lower bounds. — [Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A 372 (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.2013.0131)
- Navarro & Sadakane (TALG 2014) abstract: the "range min-max tree". Static ordinal trees take 2n + O(n/polylog n) bits with constant-time operations. Dynamic trees take 2n + O(n log log n/log n) bits with O(log n/log log n) for most operations. — [ACM TALG 10(3) (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1145/2601073)
- Liu & Yu say the state of the art is 2n + n/(log n/t)^{Ω(t)} + Õ(n^{3/4}) bits with O(t) query time, by [Pătraşcu 2008, Navarro–Sadakane 2014, Fischer–Heun 2011, Davoodi–Raman–Satti 2017]. Footnote: it was "originally stated as 2n + n/log^t n space and O(t) time for constant t". — [Liu & Yu arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05738)
- Average-case: Munro & Wild (2019) build "a succinct data structure with the optimal 1.736n+o(n) bits of space on average for random RMQ instances", settling an open problem from Davoodi et al. 2014. — [arXiv:1903.02533](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.02533). Hypersuccinct trees (Munro, Nicholson, Seelbach Benkner, Wild; ESA 2021): constant query time, worst-case 2n+o(n) bits, and 1.736n+o(n) on average for random permutations. — [arXiv:2104.13457, DOI 10.4230/LIPIcs.ESA.2021.70](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13457)
- Dynamic RMQ on the ultra-wide word RAM (Bille, Gørtz, Stordalen, Pérez López; SOFSEM 2025, arXiv v3 July 2026): O(log log log n) per query/update, with O(n/log n) words beyond the input. On the ordinary word RAM, "a tight Θ(log n/log log n) time bound on the operations is known". — [arXiv:2411.16281](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16281)
- Notable 2026 items:
  - Jo & Satti, "Encodings for Range Minimum Queries over Bounded Alphabets" (journal DOI 10.1016/j.tcs.2026.115824): near-optimal-space 1D encoding, constant time for constant alphabets. — [arXiv:2604.13350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13350)
  - Gawrychowski, Górkiewicz & Satti, 2D RMQ encoding with O(κmn(log m + log log n)) bits and O(log^{1/κ} n) query, "to appear in ESA 2026". — [arXiv:2607.04509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04509)

## Key Question 2 — Lower bounds: encoding (Catalan), indexing model, cell-probe for succinct RMQ, dynamic RMQ

### Takeaway
(i) **Encoding:** any structure that answers RMQ without A must store the Cartesian-tree shape, so it needs log₂ C_n = 2n − Θ(log n) bits (more precisely 2n − 1.5 log₂ n − O(1)). (ii) **Indexing:** with access to A, O(n/c) extra bits force Ω(c) query time, and this is tight in 1D (Brodal–Davoodi–Rao 2010/2012). (iii) **Succinct cell-probe:** with query time t, the redundancy beyond 2n must be at least n/(log n)^{Õ(t²)} (Liu–Yu STOC 2020). An improved exponent O(t log² t) is announced for SODA 2027 (Liu). (iv) **Dynamic:** Θ(log n / log log n) per operation on the word RAM. The lower bound comes from **Alstrup–Husfeldt–Rauhe's marked-ancestor bound (FOCS 1998)**, whose Proposition 2 covers "priority searching", i.e. dynamic prefix-max. Pătraşcu–Demaine's Ω(log n) is for *group* partial sums and does not directly apply to min.

### Cited Findings
**(i) Encoding / information-theoretic bound (Catalan argument)**
- Fischer–Heun: "any scheme for RMQs allows to reconstruct the Cartesian Tree by iteratively querying the scheme for the minimum … because the Cartesian Tree is binary and each binary tree is a Cartesian Tree for some input array, any scheme must use at least lg( C(2n,n)/(n+1) ) = 2n − Θ(lg n) bits". — [Fischer & Heun SICOMP preprint §1.1.2](https://ae.iti.kit.edu/1810.php)
- Liu & Yu: "The only information needed to answer all RMQ queries on A is the Cartesian tree of A … the number of such binary trees is equal to the n-th Catalan number C_n = (1/(n+1))·C(2n,n). Hence, the information theoretical space lower bound … is log₂ C_n = 2n − Θ(log n) bits". — [Liu & Yu arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05738)
- Stirling's formula as stated by Fischer: C_s = 4^s/(√π · s^{3/2}) · (1 + O(1/s)), and in particular C_s ≤ 4^s/s^{3/2}. — [Fischer thesis eq. (3.2)](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/7505/1/Fischer_Johannes.pdf); [Fischer & Heun SICOMP §3.4](https://ae.iti.kit.edu/1810.php)
- Liu's newer lower bound is stated as 2n − 1.5 log n + n/(log n)^{O(t log² t)} bits, so the additive term is explicitly −1.5 log n. — [arXiv:2111.02318](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.02318)
- Random inputs: the expected information content is ≈1.736n bits. Munro–Wild give "the optimal 1.736n+o(n) bits of space on average for random RMQ instances". — [arXiv:1903.02533](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.02533). Davoodi et al. 2014 had reached 1.919n+o(n) in expectation. — [DOI 10.1098/rsta.2013.0131](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.2013.0131)

**(ii) Indexing (systematic) model: Brodal, Davoodi & Rao**
- Abstract (ESA 2010): "every algorithm enabled to access A during the query and using O(N/c) bits additional space requires Ω(c) query time, for any c where 1 ≤ c ≤ N. This lower bound holds for any dimension. In particular, for the one dimensional version of the problem, the lower bound is tight up to a constant factor." — [BDR ESA 2010 preprint](https://cs.au.dk/~gerth/papers/esa10.pdf)
- Proof idea, in the non-uniform cell-probe model where only probes are counted. Take arrays over {0,1} with exactly one 0 in each block of size c; there are c^{n/c} of them. But there are only 2^{n/c} different indexes of n/c bits. By pigeonhole, one index is shared by (c/2)^{n/c} arrays, and some block query then needs Ω(c) probes into A. — [BDR ESA 2010 §2.1](https://cs.au.dk/~gerth/papers/esa10.pdf)
- Matching upper bound (BDR Theorem 2): O(n) preprocessing, O(c) query, O(n/c) additional bits. Split A into n/c blocks of size c, build an O(n/c)-bit encoding on the block minima, and scan at most O(1) blocks. — [BDR ESA 2010](https://cs.au.dk/~gerth/papers/esa10.pdf). Fischer–Heun cite this as showing their 2n/c(n)-bit systematic trade-off "is optimal". — [FH SICOMP](https://ae.iti.kit.edu/1810.php)
- Journal version: Algorithmica 63(4):815–830, 2012. — [DOI 10.1007/s00453-011-9499-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00453-011-9499-0). (I read only the ESA text; I assume the journal keeps the theorem.)

**(iii) Cell-probe lower bounds for succinct (encoding) RMQ**
- Liu & Yu (STOC 2020), Theorem 1: "for any data structure supporting RMQ queries using 2n + r bits of space and query time t, we must have r ≥ n/w^{O(t² log² t)}, in the cell-probe model with word-size w ≥ Ω(log n)". In the abstract: query time O(t) needs "at least 2n + n/(log n)^{Õ(t²)} space"; for constant query time "2n+n/poly log n space is necessary". It is described as "the first lower bound for this problem". — [Liu & Yu arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05738); [STOC DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3357713.3384260)
- The technique is inspired by Pătraşcu–Viola's succinct-rank lower bound (round elimination with published bits). Standard cell sampling fails for RMQ because random query answers do not almost determine the input. — [Liu & Yu arXiv §1.2–1.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05738)
- Liu (arXiv 2111.02318 v3, "to appear" at SODA 2027): space ≥ 2n − 1.5 log n + n/(log n)^{O(t log² t)} bits for query time t (w = Θ(log n), distinct integers). This improves the exponent O(t² log² t) "leaving only a polylogarithmic gap in query time from the known upper bound". — [arXiv:2111.02318](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.02318). Status: accepted per the author's comment, not yet in print as of 2026-10-31.
- Upper bound to compare with: 2n + n/(log n/t)^{Ω(t)} + Õ(n^{3/4}) bits with O(t) time. — [Liu & Yu arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05738)

**(iv) Dynamic RMQ (point updates + range-min queries)**
- Alstrup–Husfeldt–Rauhe, main bound for marked ancestor, in the cell-probe model with word size b: t_q = Ω(log n / log(t_u · b · log n)). "For logarithmic word size the bound implies a lower bound of Θ(log n/log log n) per operation". It holds for worst-case incremental/decremental and amortized fully dynamic versions. — [BRICS RS-98-7](https://www.brics.dk/RS/98/7/BRICS-RS-98-7.pdf)
- AHR98 §2.2 "Priority Search Trees" defines operations insert(x, p(x)), delete(x), and max(x) = max{p(y) | y ≤ x}. **Proposition 2:** "Every data structure for priority searching requires time Ω(log n/log log n) per operation". This makes Willard's O(log n/log log n) RAM structure optimal. — [BRICS RS-98-7](https://www.brics.dk/RS/98/7/BRICS-RS-98-7.pdf)
- Demaine & Pătraşcu's MIT 6.897 (Spring 2005) problem set: "One can solve dynamic RMQ (thus, also 3-sided queries) in O(lg n/lg lg n) per operation. For no good reason, dynamic RMQ is usually called 'priority range searching'". It shows marked ancestor → dynamic segment stabbing, which, with the Ω(lg n/lg lg n) marked-ancestor bound, makes these structures optimal. — [MIT 6.897 ps9](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.897/spring05/psets/ps9.pdf)
- Bille et al. (2024): "on the word RAM, a tight Θ(log n/log log n) time bound on the operations is known [Brodal, Davoodi, Rao, WADS 2011]". They cite AHR98, Pătraşcu–Demaine, Brodal–Chaudhuri–Radhakrishnan (SWAT 1996) and Sleator–Tarjan as the well-understood landscape. — [arXiv:2411.16281](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16281)
- Pătraşcu–Demaine's scope: the partial-sums problem "over an arbitrary group", i.e. sums (addition, with subtraction available). In the semigroup model, "Fredman gives a tight logarithmic bound". — [arXiv:cs/0502041](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0502041)

### Inferences
- Simple explanation of (i): if I can answer every RMQ, I can rebuild the Cartesian tree. Ask for the minimum of the whole array (the root), then recurse on each side. So the data structure must tell apart all C_n ≈ 4^n/(n^{1.5}√π) tree shapes. That needs log₂ C_n ≈ 2n − 1.5 log₂ n − 0.83 bits (log₂ √π ≈ 0.826, my arithmetic). Hence "2n bits are necessary, 2n+o(n) is achievable".
- Why the dynamic bound transfers: priority searching is dynamic RMQ restricted to prefix queries [1..x], with max instead of min (negate the values). Deleting a point is the same as setting its value to +∞. So dynamic RMQ with polylogarithmic update time needs Ω(log n/log log n) per query (from AHR98 Prop. 2), and Θ(log n/log log n) is achievable. **Do not cite Pătraşcu–Demaine's Ω(log n) for dynamic RMQ.** That bound is for group operations like +, and min has no inverse. Its semigroup-model Ω(log n) (Fredman) is in an algebraic model, not the word RAM.
- On the ultra-wide word RAM (w²-bit ultrawords), dynamic RMQ drops to O(log log log n) (Bille et al.). Lower bounds depend on the model.
- In the indexing model, extra space can go well below 2n bits: n/c bits with O(c) query. The 2n bound applies only when A is not available at query time. This exact confusion caused the retracted ESCAPE 2007 claim.

### Gaps
- The exact constant in Liu–Yu's Theorem 1 exponent (r ≥ n/w^{O(t² log² t)} in the PDF text vs. "Õ(t²)" in the abstract) should be read off the STOC version. The two are consistent, since Õ hides log factors.
- I did not read Brodal–Davoodi–Rao WADS 2011, so how exactly it proves a Θ(log n/log log n) bound for 1D dynamic RMQ / path minima is not checked. The tight-bound statement is from Bille et al.'s citation.
- Willard's O(log n/log log n) priority-search structure is cited only via AHR98; its bibliographic data was not verified.

## Key Question 3 — The ±1RMQ trick and the Fischer–Heun direct method: exact parameters

### Takeaway
Bender & Farach-Colton's ±1RMQ uses **blocks of size (log n)/2**, a **sparse table on the 2n/log n block minima**, and **2^{(½ log n) − 1} = O(√n) normalized block types**. Each type gets an O(log² n)-entry table, for **O(√n log² n)** total. Fischer & Heun's direct method works on arbitrary arrays. It uses **blocks of size s = (lg n)/4**: two blocks give identical in-block answers if and only if their (canonical) Cartesian trees are equal. That gives only C_s ≤ 4^s/s^{3/2} types. Each block's type is computed in **O(s) time with ballot numbers** by simulating the stack-based Cartesian-tree construction.

### Cited Findings
**Bender & Farach-Colton (LATIN 2000), as read in the author preprint**
- Notation ⟨f(n), g(n)⟩ means preprocessing time f(n) and query time g(n). Naive table: ⟨O(n²), O(1)⟩ via dynamic programming. — [preprint](https://www3.cs.stonybrook.edu/~bender/newpub/BenderFa00-lca.pdf)
- Sparse Table (ST): M[i, j] = argmin of A[i .. i+2^j−1], filled by dynamic programming in O(n log n). The query uses k = ⌊log(j−i)⌋ and compares M(i, k) with M(j−2^k+1, k), giving ⟨O(n log n), O(1)⟩. The query needs a log/floor, i.e. "finding the most significant bit of a word". "We must have one such operation … since Harel and Tarjan showed that LCA computation has a lower bound of Ω(log log n) on a pointer machine." — [preprint](https://www3.cs.stonybrook.edu/~bender/newpub/BenderFa00-lca.pdf)
- ±1RMQ: "partition A into blocks of size (log n)/2". Define A′[1 .. 2n/log n] (block minima) and B (their positions), and run ST on A′. A query splits into a suffix of i's block, the blocks in between, and a prefix of j's block. — [preprint](https://www3.cs.stonybrook.edu/~bender/newpub/BenderFa00-lca.pdf)
- Observation 5 / Lemma 6: subtracting a block's first value ("normalization") does not change RMQ answers. Normalized blocks "are specified by a ±1 vector of length (1/2·log n) − 1. There are 2^{(1/2 log n) − 1} = O(√n) such vectors". For each type, store all O(log² n) in-block answers: "a total of O(√n log² n) total preprocessing of normalized block tables, and O(1) query time". Total space and preprocessing are O(n). — [preprint](https://www3.cs.stonybrook.edu/~bender/newpub/BenderFa00-lca.pdf)
- Fischer's thesis restates the same parameters for the "Berkman–Vishkin algorithm" on H[1..2n−1]. The sparse table on H′ takes O((2n′/log n′)·log log n′) = O(n) words. The table P takes O(√n′ log² n′) words, or O(√n′ log² n′ · log n′) = o(n′) bits. The block type "is simply the binary number obtained by comparing" consecutive elements. — [Fischer thesis §3.4.1](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/7505/1/Fischer_Johannes.pdf)

**Fischer & Heun direct method (SICOMP 2011 version; thesis §3.5; CPM 2006 implementation)**
- Block size: "blocks B_1, …, B_⌈n/s⌉ of size s = (lg n)/4". Footnote: "any block size s = lg n/(2+δ) for an arbitrary constant δ > 0 would suffice, but we use δ = 2 for simplicity". A query splits into ≤ 2 in-block queries and 1 out-of-block query. — [FH SICOMP preprint §3.1](https://ae.iti.kit.edu/1810.php)
- Lemma 3.1: for blocks B_x, B_y of size s, rmq_{B_x}(i,j) = rmq_{B_y}(i,j) for all i ≤ j **iff** C^can(B_x) = C^can(B_y) (canonical Cartesian trees). So only C_s = (1/(s+1))·C(2s,s) block types are needed, with a table P[1..C_s][1..s][1..s]. — [FH SICOMP §3.2](https://ae.iti.kit.edu/1810.php)
- Algorithm 1 (block type in O(s)): keep the rightmost path R of the partial Cartesian tree as a stack, with R[1] = −∞, q ← s and N ← 0. For i = 1..s: while R[q+i−s] > B_x[i], do N ← N + C_{(s−i) q} ("add number of skipped paths") and q ← q−1. Then push B_x[i]. Return N, a number in [0, C_s − 1]. — [FH SICOMP Alg. 1, Lemma 3.2](https://ae.iti.kit.edu/1810.php)
- Ballot numbers: C_00 = 1; C_pq = C_p(q−1) + C_(p−1)q if 0 ≤ p ≤ q ≠ 0; C_pq = 0 otherwise. Closed form C_pq = ((q−p+1)/(q+1))·C(p+q, p), so C_ss = C_s (Catalan). Storing them costs O(lg³ n) bits. — [FH SICOMP §3.2 and §3.4](https://ae.iti.kit.edu/1810.php); thesis §3.5.1 "A New Code for Binary Trees" credits ballot numbers to Knuth (2006). — [Fischer thesis](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/7505/1/Fischer_Johannes.pdf)
- Table size: C_s ≤ 4^s/s^{3/2} rows. Storing all answers naively would take O((4^s/s^{3/2})·s²·lg s) bits. With Alstrup et al.'s trick (one s-bit vector per start position), |P| = O((4^s/s^{3/2})·s·s) = O(√n lg n) = o(n/lg n) bits. — [FH SICOMP §3.4](https://ae.iti.kit.edu/1810.php)
- Out-of-block queries use "a two-level storage scheme due to Sadakane". s contiguous blocks are grouped into super-blocks of s′ = s² elements, with sparse-table-like tables M″ (super-block level) and M′ (block level within super-blocks). — [FH SICOMP §3.3](https://ae.iti.kit.edu/1810.php)
- Resulting systematic scheme (Lemma 3.6): ⟨O(n·t_A), O(t_A)⟩ time and |A| + 2n − Θ(n lg lg n/lg n) + O(n/lg n) bits. The exact leading constant of the negative term was garbled in my text extraction, so check the PDF. The paper notes that "the final space on top of A is asymptotically less than 2n bits". The final Theorem 3.7 groups c(n) elements to reach 2n/c(n) − Θ(n lg lg n/(c(n) lg n)) bits. — [FH SICOMP §3.4–3.5](https://ae.iti.kit.edu/1810.php)
- The CPM 2006 link: Fischer's thesis says its "non-succinct version" is "a straightforward combination of the Berkman-Vishkin algorithm with our new preprocessing scheme for short queries … The block-size was set to s = log n/4 … This implementation corresponds to the paper presented at CPM 2006". It also notes there is "no need for an intermediate construction of the Canonical Cartesian Tree, thereby also avoiding the input doubling" (the 2n−1 Euler tour). — [Fischer thesis §3.8](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/7505/1/Fischer_Johannes.pdf)
- Teaching resource: Stanford CS166 "Range Minimum Queries, Part II" introduces Cartesian trees and assigns the Fischer–Heun CPM 2006 paper as reading. — [Stanford CS166 lecture 01 page](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs166/lectures/01/)

### Inferences
- Why the block sizes work (simple arithmetic):
  - ±1 trick, b = ½ log n: number of types is 2^{b−1} < 2^{½ log n} = √n. Each table has O(b²) = O(log² n) entries, so the total is O(√n log² n) = o(n).
  - Fischer–Heun, s = ¼ log n: C_s < 4^s = 4^{(log n)/4} = 2^{(log n)/2} = √n. The constant ¼ (rather than ½) is needed because the Catalan count grows like 4^s rather than 2^s.
- Simple explanation for students: "Blocks that look the same to RMQ" means blocks with the same Cartesian-tree shape. The ±1 case is a special case where the shape is fixed by the up/down pattern. Ballot numbers just give each shape a serial number from 0 to C_s − 1, computed while the stack-based construction runs, so no tree is ever built.
- Note for the presenter: CS166-style lectures often encode the stack push/pop sequence as a 2s-bit number instead of using ballot numbers. That is a simplification of teaching, not the paper's method (I did not verify the exact CS166 encoding).

### Gaps
- I could not read the CPM 2006 PDF itself. That ballot-number typing and the exact superblock size appeared already in CPM 2006 (rather than only in ESCAPE 2007/SICOMP 2011/thesis) is UNVERIFIED. The thesis confirms only that the CPM 2006 implementation used s = log n/4 with the new short-query scheme.
- The exact text of Bender & Farach-Colton's LNCS version may differ from the May 2000 preprint I read, including its reference list.

## Key Question 4 — The RMQ ↔ LCA equivalence: precise statements, linear-time Cartesian tree, Euler tour of length 2n−1

### Takeaway
The two reductions are: **LCA → ±1RMQ**, via the Euler tour E[1..2n−1], the level array L[1..2n−1] and the representative array R[1..n], giving ⟨f(2n−1)+O(n), g(2n−1)+O(1)⟩. And **RMQ → LCA**, via the Cartesian tree, with RMQ_A(i,j) = LCA_C(i,j). Both reductions take linear time, so the problems are linearly equivalent. The Cartesian tree is built in O(n) time by walking up the rightmost path (a stack): each element enters and leaves the path at most once.

### Cited Findings
- Lemma 3 (Bender–Farach-Colton): "If there is an ⟨f(n), g(n)⟩-time solution for RMQ, then there is an ⟨f(2n−1)+O(n), g(2n−1)+O(1)⟩-time solution for LCA". Observation 4: "The LCA of nodes u and v is the shallowest node encountered between the visits to u and to v during a depth first search traversal of T". Construction: E[1..2n−1] is the Euler tour, L[i] the level of E[i], and R[i] the first occurrence of node i. Then LCA_T(u,v) = E[RMQ_L(R[u], R[v])]. — [Bender & Farach-Colton preprint](https://www3.cs.stonybrook.edu/~bender/newpub/BenderFa00-lca.pdf)
- Why the length is 2n−1: "we start at the root and subsequently output a node each time we traverse an edge. We traverse each of the n−1 edges twice". Adjacent levels differ by exactly ±1 "because, for any two adjacent elements in an Euler tour, one is always the parent of the other". — [Bender & Farach-Colton preprint](https://www3.cs.stonybrook.edu/~bender/newpub/BenderFa00-lca.pdf)
- Lemma 7 / Claim 7A: "If there is an ⟨O(n), O(1)⟩ solution for LCA, then there is an ⟨O(n), O(1)⟩ solution for RMQ", and RMQ_A(i,j) = LCA_C(i,j). The LCA k "is the first node that separates i and j … it is between them because it splits them, and it is minimal because it is the first element to do so". — [preprint](https://www3.cs.stonybrook.edu/~bender/newpub/BenderFa00-lca.pdf)
- Linear-time construction: "node i+1 will belong to the rightmost path of C_{i+1}, so we climb up the rightmost path of C_i until finding the position where i+1 belongs. Each comparison either adds an element to the rightmost path or removes one, and each node can only join the rightmost path and leave it once. Thus the total time to build C_n is O(n)." — [preprint](https://www3.cs.stonybrook.edu/~bender/newpub/BenderFa00-lca.pdf). Fischer attributes this construction algorithm to Gabow et al. (1984). — [Fischer thesis §3.2](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/7505/1/Fischer_Johannes.pdf)
- Ties: Fischer defines the canonical Cartesian tree with the order A[i] ≺ A[j] iff A[i] < A[j], or A[i] = A[j] and i < j. This makes the tree and RMQ answers unique (leftmost minimum). — [Fischer thesis Def. 3.1](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/7505/1/Fischer_Johannes.pdf). Liu & Yu use the same leftmost-minimum convention. — [arXiv:2004.05738](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05738)
- The Cartesian tree definition (root = minimum, left/right subtrees = Cartesian trees of the left/right subarrays) is "due to Vuillemin (1980)". — [Fischer thesis](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/7505/1/Fischer_Johannes.pdf); Vuillemin's abstract describes "a simple geometrical construction" yielding efficient searching and list-manipulation algorithms. — [CACM DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/358841.358852)
- "RMQ-problem and the LCA-problem are linearly equivalent, in the sense that an instance of one problem can be transformed into an instance of the other in linear time." The drawback is "the doubling of the input when going from A to H; i.e., H has size n′ := 2n − 1". — [Fischer thesis §3.4.1](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/7505/1/Fischer_Johannes.pdf)

### Inferences
- Full pipeline for general RMQ in Bender–Farach-Colton: array → Cartesian tree (n nodes) → Euler tour depths (2n−1 entries, ±1) → ±1RMQ. The "round trip" through LCA is what Fischer–Heun avoid by typing blocks directly by Cartesian-tree shape.
- Simple explanation: in a DFS walk, between first visiting u and first visiting v you must pass through their lowest common ancestor, and never above it. So "shallowest node on the walk between u and v" equals the LCA. In the Cartesian tree, the first (highest) node whose index separates i and j is the smallest value between them.

## Key Question 5 — Model assumptions: word-RAM, word size Θ(log n), "O(n) space" in words vs bits; systematic vs non-systematic

### Takeaway
All classic LCA/RMQ results assume a **word-RAM with w = Ω(log n)-bit words** and O(1)-time arithmetic and logic. Harel–Tarjan state "Ω(log n) bits per word"; lower bounds use the cell-probe model with w = Θ(log n). "O(n) space" in the classic papers means **O(n) words = O(n log n) bits**. Succinct results count **bits**. One must also separate **systematic/indexing** schemes (A kept and read at query time; space counted on top of |A|) from **non-systematic/encoding** schemes (A discarded; 2n − Θ(log n) bits necessary).

### Cited Findings
- Fischer–Heun: "We work with the standard word-RAM model of computation (which is also the model used in all LCA- and RMQ-schemes cited in this article), where it is assumed that we can do arithmetic and logical operations on w-bit wide words in O(1) time, and w = Ω(lg n)". Throughout, "space is measured in bits". — [FH SICOMP preprint](https://ae.iti.kit.edu/1810.php)
- Fischer–Heun: the problem with Gabow et al.'s solution and its simplifications "is their space consumption of O(n lg n) bits, as they store O(n) words occupying ⌈lg n⌉ bits each". — [FH SICOMP preprint](https://ae.iti.kit.edu/1810.php)
- Fischer–Heun: following Gál & Miltersen, schemes are "systematic and non-systematic (also called indexing and encoding data structures, respectively)". Systematic schemes "must store the input array A verbatim"; non-systematic ones "must be able to obtain their final answer without consulting the array". — [FH SICOMP preprint §1.1](https://ae.iti.kit.edu/1810.php)
- Harel–Tarjan's O(1)-query algorithm is for "a random access machine with uniform cost measure (and a bound of Ω(log n) on the number of bits per word)". A pointer machine needs Ω(log log n) per query. — [DOI 10.1137/0213024](https://doi.org/10.1137/0213024)
- Liu–Yu: "It is a standard assumption that the word-size is at least Ω(log n), since the query answer is an index, which requires log n bits to describe". The cell-probe model counts only memory accesses, and preprocessing may take unbounded time. — [arXiv:2004.05738](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05738)
- Bender–Farach-Colton: the ST query needs ⌊log(j−i)⌋, i.e. the most-significant-bit operation, which "has a very fast table lookup solution". — [preprint](https://www3.cs.stonybrook.edu/~bender/newpub/BenderFa00-lca.pdf)
- Why encodings matter in practice: RMQ is often used on LCP arrays stored compressed, where reading A costs Ω(lg^ε n). A systematic scheme would then lose O(1) query time. — [FH SICOMP preprint §1.1](https://ae.iti.kit.edu/1810.php)
- Dynamic RMQ in a stronger model: the UWRAM adds w²-bit "ultrawords" with arithmetic, boolean and scattered memory access, and gets O(log log log n) per operation. — [arXiv:2411.16281](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16281)

### Inferences
- Worked example for the seminar: n = 2³² and w = 32. A "linear-space" sparse-table/±1 structure of a few words per element uses about 4–16 bytes per element. An encoding of 2n + o(n) bits uses about 0.25 bytes per element, plus lower-order terms. Fischer's thesis reports its succinct implementation at "≤ 7/8·n bytes" for n ≤ 2³². The fraction is garbled in my text extraction, so check it in the PDF before quoting.
- A clean slide summary of the three regimes:
  - (a) O(n) words, O(1) query — 1984 onward.
  - (b) Systematic: |A| + O(n/c) bits, Θ(c) query — optimal, BDR 2010 / FH 2011.
  - (c) Encoding: 2n + o(n) bits, O(1) query — Fischer 2010 / FH 2011. The redundancy has to be n/polylog n (Liu–Yu 2020).
